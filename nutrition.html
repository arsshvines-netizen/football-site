Fuel your game

Simple food and drink habits to give you energy for training, help you recover, and support a growing body. No diets and no counting. Just eat enough, eat a mix, and drink water.

Important: supplements, energy drinks and protein powders are not needed for young players. Normal, balanced meals give you everything you need.

Eat enough, eat balanced

You're training and growing, so your body needs plenty of fuel. Eat regular meals and snacks, and don't skip meals, especially breakfast.

Carbohydrates

Your main fuel. Gives you energy to run and think. Rice, pasta, bread, potatoes, oats, chapati, noodles, cereals.

Protein

Builds and repairs muscles. Beans, lentils, eggs, milk, yoghurt, chicken, fish, tofu, paneer, nuts.

Fruit and vegetables

Vitamins and minerals to keep you healthy and help you recover. Aim for lots of different colours each day.

Healthy fats

Needed in small amounts for energy and health. Nuts, seeds, avocado, olive oil, oily fish.

Calcium and iron

Calcium (milk, yoghurt, cheese, fortified plant milks) for strong bones. Iron (beans, lentils, leafy greens, meat, eggs) for energy.

Treats are OK

No food is "banned". Enjoy treats sometimes, as part of a normal, varied diet.

    Hydration

    • Drink water regularly through the day, not just at training.
    • Before: have a drink of water in the hour or two before training.
    • During: take sips of water at every break.
    • After: keep drinking to replace what you lost in sweat.
    • Drink more on hot days or when you sweat a lot.
    • Water is the best choice. Milk is also a great drink after training.

    The pee colour check

    A simple way to check if you're drinking enough is to look at the colour of your urine (pee) when you go to the toilet.

    • Pale straw: well hydrated 👍
    • Yellow: drink some water
    • Dark yellow: drink water now

    Some foods and vitamins can change the colour. If you're ever worried, talk to a parent or doctor.

      Everyone is different

      Every young person is different and still growing. How much you need to eat changes with your age, growth and how active you are. If you have allergies, a medical condition, special dietary needs, or any worries about food or your body, talk to a parent and ask a doctor or registered dietitian.
