Why fitness matters in football

Run all game

A match has lots of short sprints, jogging and walking. Stamina helps you keep playing well until the final whistle.

Win the race

Being quick over the first few steps helps you get to the ball first, attack space and recover when you lose it.

Stay strong and safe

Strong legs and core help you hold off players, keep your balance and lower your chance of getting injured.

    Speed and agility

    Speed work only helps when you are fresh. Do it near the start of a session (after the warm-up), keep sprints short, and rest fully between each one. If you are slowing down, stop.

      Strength and stability

      Start with the beginner version. Only move on when you can do every rep with perfect form. Rest 60 seconds between sets. 2 strength sessions a week is plenty.

      Strength exercises with beginner, intermediate and advanced versions
      ExerciseBeginnerIntermediateAdvanced

      Strength training with your own bodyweight is a normal, safe part of training for young people when the technique is good. Ask a coach to check your form, especially when you try a new exercise.

      Endurance

      Football is a stop-start game, so intervals (hard, then easy, then hard again) are better than long slow runs. Small-sided games (like 3v3 or 4v4) are a great way to build stamina with the ball.

      Mobility and cool-down

      After training, walk or jog easily for 2-3 minutes, then stretch gently. Never bounce, and never stretch into pain. You should only feel a mild pull.

        Rest, sleep and recovery

        Rest days

        Your body gets stronger between sessions, not during them. Have at least 1-2 full rest days every week, and never train hard the day before a match.

        Sleep

        Sleep experts recommend 9-12 hours a night for 12-year-olds and 8-10 hours for 13-18 year-olds. Put screens away before bed and try to sleep at the same time each night.

        Listen to your body

        Feeling very tired, sore for days, grumpy or not enjoying football? These can be signs you need more rest. Tell your coach or parent and take an easier week.

        Injury prevention and safety

        Golden rules

        • Always warm up before training and matches.
        • Stop if there is pain. Never train through pain. Being a bit tired is normal; sharp pain is not.
        • Train on safe surfaces: check for holes, glass, wet or icy patches, and use the right boots.
        • Drink water before, during and after training.
        • Tell a coach or parent if you feel unwell, dizzy or injured.
        • See a doctor if pain lasts more than a few days, keeps coming back, or there is swelling.
        • Build up slowly. Don't suddenly double how much you train. Growing bodies need time.
